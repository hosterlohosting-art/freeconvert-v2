How to Make a GIF from Photos?

  1. Drop your photos into the dotted drag-and-drop zone, or click Choose Photos. You can add up to 20 images.
  2. Drag the frames into order with the up and down arrows, then set the frame speed, width, and whether the GIF loops.
  3. Click Make GIF. Each photo becomes one frame of the animation. Your files never leave your browser.
  4. Watch the preview, check the size, then download the GIF. Keep your original photos until you are happy with the loop.

📝 GIF Maker Glossary & Standards

Frame Delay: How long each photo shows, in milliseconds. 500 ms (half a second) per photo is a calm pace; 100 ms is fast and jumpy.

Colour Palette: A GIF can only hold 256 colours in each frame. Photos with smooth gradients may look banded — simple, bold pictures look best.

Loop: Whether the GIF plays again from the start when it finishes. Looping on is right for chats and memes; off plays once and stops on the last photo.

What is GIF Maker?

GIF Maker turns your photos into one animated GIF. Each photo becomes a frame, shown for as long as you set, in the order you choose — then the animation loops or plays once, your choice. It is built for users who need a quick, free, and private way to make short animations without installing desktop software. GIF Maker format notes

A GIF holds at most 256 colours in each frame, and it has no sound. That is why bold, simple photos look best — smooth gradients and dark photos can look banded. Each photo is cropped to the first photo's shape, so use photos with the same shape when you can. An animated GIF used as a frame only gives its first picture. Transparent parts of PNG images are filled white in the GIF. When the GIF comes out too big, use fewer photos first, then a smaller width.

⚠️ System Constraints & Limitations

This tool makes GIFs in your browser with gif.js, so it has real limits. You can add up to 20 photos, each 50 MB or smaller — more frames need too much browser memory and can freeze the tab. The GIF width is capped at 640 pixels. A GIF holds only 256 colours per frame, so smooth gradients can look banded, and it has no sound. Transparent parts of PNG images are filled white. Each photo is cropped to the first photo's shape. Making a GIF takes a while when there are many frames — the progress bar shows how far along it is. A long GIF can still come out several megabytes — check the size before sharing.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

❓ Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. The whole GIF is built in your browser with gif.js. Your photos never leave your device.

❓ Which photos can I use?

PNG, JPG, WebP, and GIF files. An animated GIF used as a frame only gives its first picture.

❓ How many photos can I add?

Up to 20 photos, each up to 50 MB. Fewer, smaller photos make a smaller GIF that loads fast.

❓ Why does making the GIF take a while?

Each photo is reduced to 256 colours and packed frame by frame, in your browser. More photos take longer — the progress bar shows how far along it is.

❓ Why is my GIF so big?

A GIF stores every frame as a full picture, so size grows with the number of photos, the width, and the quality setting. Use fewer photos first, then a smaller width.
