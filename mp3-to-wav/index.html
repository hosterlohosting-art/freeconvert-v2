MP3 to WAV format notes

WAV is uncompressed: it keeps every sound sample, so nothing is lost in the conversion itself — but it cannot bring back detail the MP3 already threw away. A 3-minute song becomes a WAV of about 30 MB, roughly 10 times the MP3. Use WAV when a program needs it, not to make the sound better. When quality matters, keep a backup of the original MP3 and listen to the WAV before deleting anything.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

❓ Is my audio file uploaded to a server?

No. The whole conversion runs in your browser with ffmpeg.wasm. Your audio file never leaves your device.

❓ Which audio files can I convert?

MP3 files. The result is always a WAV file — uncompressed 16-bit sound at 44.1 kHz, the standard CD format.

❓ Why does the first conversion take so long?

The first conversion downloads the ffmpeg converter code (about 24 MB) into your browser. After that first download, later conversions start right away. Short clips convert fastest.

❓ Why is the WAV file so much bigger than the MP3?

WAV is uncompressed: it stores every sound sample exactly, while MP3 throws away detail to stay small. A 3-minute song becomes a WAV of about 30 MB. The bigger file is normal — it is the price of uncompressed sound.

❓ How big can my MP3 file be?

Up to 100 MB per file. Bigger files need too much browser memory and can crash the tab, so the tool stops them with a clear message.
