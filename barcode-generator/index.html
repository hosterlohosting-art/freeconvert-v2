📄 Barcode Formats Glossary & Definitions

CODE128: The most flexible barcode. It takes letters, numbers, and symbols, so it works for almost anything — serial numbers, order IDs, asset tags, and file references.

EAN-13: The retail barcode used on products sold outside the US and Canada. It is 13 digits: 12 digits you type, plus a check digit the tool adds for you.

UPC-A: The retail barcode used on products sold in the US and Canada. It is 12 digits: 11 digits you type, plus a check digit the tool adds for you.

CODE39: A simple barcode for labels. It takes capital letters, numbers, and only these symbols: - . space $ / + %. It is wider than CODE128 for the same text.

ITF-14: The barcode printed on shipping cartons and cases. It is 14 digits: 13 digits you type, plus a check digit the tool adds for you.

Check digit: The last digit of an EAN-13, UPC-A, or ITF-14 code. It is calculated from the other digits, and scanners use it to catch typing mistakes. This tool calculates it for you — you never type it yourself.

What is a barcode?

A barcode is a picture of black bars and white gaps that a scanner reads as a number or a short piece of text. Each bar width means something: the pattern of wide and narrow bars is the code, written in a way a machine can read. This tool draws real barcodes with the standard encoding for each format — including the check digit for EAN-13, UPC-A, and ITF-14 — so a phone camera or a shop scanner can read them.

Honest limits — read before you print

A barcode is only as good as its input. Wrong digits make a barcode that scans to the wrong number, so always check the number printed under the bars against what you typed. EAN-13 and UPC-A are for real product codes: a made-up number will scan, but it is not a valid retail code — shops will reject it, because real product numbers come from GS1. Print at 100 percent scale and keep the barcode at least 30 mm wide for EAN-13, or scanners will struggle. Dark bars on a light background scan best; a coloured background or a stretched image can make a barcode unreadable.
