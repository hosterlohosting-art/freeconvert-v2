Internet Speed Test Glossary

Download speed: How fast data comes down to your device, measured in Mbps (megabits per second). A 25 Mbps connection downloads a 25-megabit file in about one second — enough for HD video. 100 Mbps handles 4K.

Ping (latency): How long a tiny message takes to go to the server and back, measured in milliseconds (ms). Under 30 ms feels instant for calls and games; over 100 ms you will notice lag.

Mbps: Megabits per second — the unit internet speed is sold in. Note the small "b": 100 Mbps is about 12.5 MB (megabytes) per second, because 8 bits make 1 byte.

What is Internet Speed Test?

An internet speed test measures how fast your connection really is, right now. This one measures two things: your download speed — how fast data reaches your device — and your ping — how quickly the server answers. Both are measured live in your browser: the test downloads a real file from the CDN and times it, then reports the speed. Nothing is guessed or simulated.

Use it before an important video call, when a stream keeps buffering, or when you want to check whether your connection matches the speed your internet plan promises. Run it on Wi-Fi and, if you can, on a wired connection too — the difference tells you whether your Wi-Fi is the bottleneck.

What the numbers mean

Download speed is the headline number, but ping matters just as much for calls and games. A 200 Mbps connection with a 150 ms ping feels worse in a video call than a 50 Mbps connection with a 20 ms ping. Test at different times: evenings are usually slower because your neighbours are online too. And test on a cable if you can — Wi-Fi alone can cut your speed in half through walls.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is this speed test free?

Yes. It is completely free, with no sign-up and no app to install.

What does this speed test measure?

Your real download speed and your ping to the Cloudflare CDN (cdnjs). It does not measure upload speed — this site has no server that accepts uploads.

Why is my speed lower than my internet plan promises?

Plans advertise the maximum speed under ideal conditions. Wi-Fi, other devices on your network, evening congestion, and the distance to the test server all lower the real number.
