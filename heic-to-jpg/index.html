Image Formats Glossary & Definitions

PNG (Portable Network Graphics): A lossless compressed raster format that records pixel data exactly as created. It supports full alpha transparency, making it the perfect choice for logos, typography, and clean graphic assets.

JPG / JPEG (Joint Photographic Experts Group): A lossy compressed raster format optimized for photograph color depth. It compresses file weights aggressively by discarding visually subtle variations, speeding up webpage loading.

WebP (Web Picture Format): Google's next-gen lossy and lossless image standard. It compresses image weights by 30% more than traditional JPEGs, directly boosting Google Core Web Vitals rankings.

SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics): An XML-based vector graphics standard. Rather than storing a grid of pixels, it stores geometric paths, enabling graphics to scale to infinite sizes with zero blurring.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why does Apple use HEIC format instead of JPG?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) uses advanced compression to store photos at half the file size of JPG while maintaining identical image quality. However, it is not supported on older platforms.

Does this conversion delete the original photo from my iPhone?

No, your original HEIC photos remain completely untouched on your device. The tool simply processes a copy in memory and outputs a fresh JPG for download.

Are my high-resolution photos sent to any server?

Absolutely not. All HEIC parsing, decoding, and JPG rendering are carried out locally on your computer using client-side libraries.

Does the HEIC to JPG converter preserve metadata?

No. The converter transfers the photo itself — its pixels, dimensions, and colors — but camera metadata such as EXIF settings is not carried over to the JPG. Keep your original HEIC file if you need that data.

Can I bulk convert HEIC photos?

Yes, batch selection is supported, allowing you to convert groups of iPhone HEIF photos simultaneously.
