Audio Formats Glossary & Definitions

MP3: The most widely supported audio format. It throws away some sound detail to make files small — a 3-minute song is about 3 MB at 128 kbps. It plays on almost every phone, computer, car stereo, and website.

WAV: Uncompressed audio. Every sound sample is stored exactly, so WAV files are big — about 10 MB per minute — but they lose nothing. Music editors and CD burners usually want WAV.

OGG, M4A, and FLAC: OGG (Vorbis) is a free, open format used by games and some recorders. M4A (AAC) is what iPhones use for voice memos. FLAC keeps every sound detail, so its files are big. Many players and websites do not accept any of them — that is why people convert them to MP3.

Why is the WAV file so much bigger than the MP3?

WAV is uncompressed: it stores every sound sample exactly, while MP3 throws away detail to stay small. A 3-minute song becomes a WAV of about 30 MB. The bigger file is normal — it is the price of uncompressed sound.
